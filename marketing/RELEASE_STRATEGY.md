# LAST CALL — Positioning, Marketing & Release Strategy
*Objective: high ratings AND box office. The plan treats critical acclaim as a
marketing line item — for a $4.5M comedy, the reviews ARE the media buy.*

---

## 1. POSITIONING

**One-line position:** *Fargo* warmth × *It's Always Sunny* escalation, packaged
as the true-crime docuseries America already binge-watches.

**Comps (the honest ones, for financiers):**
- Tonal: *Fargo*, *In Bruges* (crime-comedy with a real heart)
- Format: *American Vandal*, *I, Tonya* (mock-doc frame audiences love)
- Business model: theatrical breakout indie comedies driven by word of mouth
  from platform release + festival heat, not P&A spend

**The pitch sentence for buyers:** the only crime comedy this year in which the
central heist is *giving the money back* — a premise that describes itself in
one breath and hasn't been done.

## 2. AUDIENCE QUADRANTS & WHY EACH SHOWS UP

- **25–45 comedy-internet natives:** Robinson/O'Malley/Mantzoukas clip economy;
  the docuseries-parody format is their native tongue.
- **45+ prestige-TV audience:** Offerman, Simmons, Martindale; the Fargo tone;
  "the true-crime parody" hook lands hardest with the demo that watches the
  most true crime.
- **Couples / date night:** crime-comedy is the most reliably co-watchable
  genre; the Ricky/Crystal thread gives the film a genuine (weird) love story.
- **The soft-R dividend (locked in DECISIONS):** no glamorized vice, no nudity —
  "take your dad" word of mouth is the cheapest media that exists.

## 3. FESTIVAL & RELEASE PLAN

**Phase 1 — Festival (the reviews engine):**
Premiere target: SXSW (comedy-native, breakout-friendly, cast can work the
premiere as a comedy event) with TIFF Midnight/Special Presentations as the
alternate. Goal: 90%+ on the review aggregators out of the gate + an audience
award — the two assets every downstream negotiation quotes.

**Phase 2 — Platform theatrical (weeks 1–2):**
Limited opening (NY/LA/Chicago/Austin) PLUS a symbolic wide-in-Ohio move:
Toledo, Cleveland, Columbus open day-and-date with NY/LA. "The movie opened in
Toledo before Manhattan" is a free national press story and the campaign's
authenticity proof.

**Phase 3 — Expansion (weeks 3–6):**
Expand on exit-poll strength. The film's structural advantages for legs:
96-minute runtime (extra daily showtime), A-range CinemaScore engineering
(protected reveals, ending that sends audiences out warm), and clip-driven
discovery that doesn't decay like a TV buy.

**Counter-programming instinct:** open against a franchise tentpole, not against
another comedy. Comedies with distinct voices historically thrive as the
alternative choice, and LAST CALL's demo is precisely the audience not going to
the robot movie.

## 4. THE CAMPAIGN'S THREE ENGINES

**Engine A — The docuseries pretends to be real.**
All paid and social creative ships in *Rust Belt Outlaws* packaging: character
"case file" posts, evidence-board graphics, talking-head clips released like a
streaming doc's promo cycle. Two weeks before the trailer, seed an unbranded
"RUST BELT OUTLAWS — this March" teaser that looks 100% like a real true-crime
doc; the reveal that it's a comedy IS the trailer-launch story.

**Engine B — Kevin's diss tracks are real.**
Every diss track in the film drops on streaming under "Kevin" as actual singles,
in production-quality order ("Dale and Ricky, Broke and Sticky" in clipped mono
→ "Storage War Criminals" → "Snack-Back" in $11,000-microphone stereo). The
fidelity-upgrade gag becomes a discoverable easter egg; the tracks are the
trailer bed; TikTok does the rest. Cost: in-house. Value: a media channel we own.

**Engine C — Mr. Business.**
The raccoon runs the social accounts. Deadpan single-image posts, market
commentary (he predicts economic downturns), zero explanation, tiny vest
merchandising held for week of release. Mascot campaigns are the highest
share-per-dollar asset an indie comedy can own, and this one is load-bearing in
the film anyway.

**Ecosystem tie-in (Pantheon/LuxeForge):** "Operation Take-Backsies" onboarding
skin — a gamified hero's-journey where every quest is politely returning
something; codename generator ("Operation ______") as the shareable unit.
Ship it with the trailer, not the release — it's an awareness asset.

## 5. RATINGS PROTECTION (the review strategy is creative, not PR)

The five decisions already locked that critics reward, restated as strategy:
1. Grief played straight under the comedy (the Act 3 floorboard pull) — this is
   what separates 90%+ comedies from 60% ones.
2. The Crystal scene protected — the film has a thesis and a monologue critics
   can quote.
3. The convergence grammar — reviewers get a *filmmaking* idea to write about,
   not just jokes (form-as-joke is catnip for the top critics).
4. Soft-R discipline — no critic docks it for cruelty or glamor.
5. 96 minutes — "doesn't overstay its welcome" appears in a third of positive
   comedy reviews. Give them the sentence.

**CinemaScore engineering:** the last five minutes send the audience out on the
warmest image in the film (the one-take, the chip, "We own the road now") plus
a hard laugh over the cut to black (Paczki's two-for-one). Exit warm + exit
laughing = the A– that buys week three.

## 6. WHAT WE MEASURE

Festival: aggregator score at 20+ reviews, audience-award placement, sale terms.
Release: per-screen average week 1, second-weekend drop (<45% target),
CinemaScore (A– target), clip velocity (Engine A/B/C share counts), Ohio
per-screen vs. NY/LA (the authenticity bet, measured).
