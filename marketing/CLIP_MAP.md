# LAST CALL — Short-Form Clip Map & Social Capture Plan
*The innovation: social content is photographed, not scavenged. Every `[CLIP]`
tag in the shot lists resolves to a planned asset here, captured on set by a
dedicated rig, released on a calendar engineered around the theatrical dates.*

---

## 1. THE CAPTURE SYSTEM (on-set, zero performance cost)

- **The Social B-Body:** one mirrorless body on a cage, operated by the second
  AC trainee or a dedicated social shooter, rolling 9:16 verticals ONLY on
  setups tagged `[CLIP]`. It rolls WITH the A-camera take — never its own take,
  never an extra setup, never a minute of overtime. If the vertical and the
  film's frame conflict, the film wins and the clip is recomposed from the
  4.6K A-cam scan in post (center-crop 9:16 is the fallback, not the plan).
- **Sound:** clips take the production mix. The film's sound design IS the
  brand (thermos-gunshot, ambience dropouts, mono Kevin) — no music beds
  slapped on in the social edit. Ever.
- **The doc unit doubles:** the talking-head setups are already
  perfect verticals when reframed — shoot every talking head twice, once in
  scene (16:9) and one 20-second "extra question" take composed for 9:16,
  written specifically as social material (new, non-spoiler questions:
  "Describe Dale in one word." Paczki: "...Present.").
- **Cost:** one body + media + one operator ≈ rounding error inside the camera
  package; already absorbable in the BUDGET_SCHEDULE camera line.

## 3. THE PROTECTED REGISTER (restated as social law)

No Duke, no Tommy, no ball pit, no garage scene, no goose-lady value, no
bridge interiors, no chip. If a clip would need context from a protected
scene, the clip does not exist. Marketing that spends the heart buys week one
and forfeits weeks two through six — the entire legs strategy is the audience
discovering the floorboards in the theater.

## 4. INNOVATION LEDGER (each campaign beat must beat the last — running list)

1. **Unbranded fake-doc drop** (T-minus 10 weeks): a 45-sec *Rust Belt
   Outlaws* promo indistinguishable from a real streamer's true-crime slate,
   placed like one. The comedy reveal arrives with the real teaser two weeks
   later — the bait-and-switch IS the story the press writes.
2. **The Evidence Board** (T-8): interactive image — the crayon map, the
   security-cam still (red blink), the pull-tab, the tiny vest receipt.
   Followers "solve" the case pre-release; every artifact is from the film.
3. **Kevin's singles ladder** (T-8 → T-1): fidelity-upgrade gag as
   discoverable easter egg; final track drops with credits audio week of.
4. **Local-first premiere** (release week): Toledo/Cleveland/Columbus
   day-and-date with NY/LA, cast at the Toledo screen, Flapjack-style pancake
   pop-up outside. National story: "It opened in Toledo before Manhattan."
5. **The Codename Generator** (Pantheon/LuxeForge tie-in, T-2): shareable
   "Operation ______" generator; onboarding skin where every hero-quest is
   politely returning something. Awareness asset, ships with trailer 2.
6. **Post-opening pivot — "Thursdays"** (week 2+): the two-for-one Paczki
   button clip releases ONLY after opening weekend, converting the film's
   final joke into the repeat-viewing hook ("Thursdays" discount showtimes
   with exhibitors where negotiable).

## 5. MEASUREMENT (per clip, weekly)

Completion rate (premise clips), share/duet rate (sound-memes), follower
delta on the Kevin and Mr. Business accounts, search lift on "last call
movie," and — the number that matters — trailer-view-to-ticket conversion
window over release weeks 0–2. Underperforming clip archetypes get replaced
from inventory; the register never gets raided to chase a number.
