# LAST CALL — Casting Breakdown
*Master Director Connector v1.0. Two tracks offered for Dale (name-comedian vs.
character-actor) since that decision shapes the whole board. My lean is stated;
the room decides.*

Casting law for this film: **nobody is allowed to know they're funny.** Every
audition side is played dead straight; anyone who winks is a pass. We are casting
for deadpan endurance (Dale), sincere delusion (Ricky), weaponized warmth (Larry),
and audit-grade stillness (Crystal).

---

## DALE KOWALSKI (54) — deadpan alcoholic "mastermind"

**Track 1 — Character-actor deadpan (MY LEAN: truer to the D-minus energy, and the
sober-Dale turn in Act 3 needs an actor who can be quietly frightening):**
1. **Shea Whigham** — the face of a foreclosed house, per the character bible.
   Carries thirty years of bad nights without acting them. The Act 3 horror-beat
   through the truck window is free.
2. **Michael Shannon** — the scariest possible sober Dale; the comedy comes from
   absolute refusal to play comedy. Risk: tilts the tone 5% darker (may be a
   feature).
3. **Sam Rockwell** — most physically gifted option; the ball-pit flask hold would
   be devastating. Slight fight against his natural charm.
4. **Walton Goggins** — deadpan with grief right under the enamel; great flask
   actor. Watch the Southern lean vs. Midwest.
5. **Nick Offerman** — bridges both tracks: bankable AND authentically Midwest
   deadpan. If the producers need one name to close financing, it's this one.

**Track 2 — Name-comedian deadpan (bankable, tonal risk):**
1. **Bill Burr** — Rust Belt authenticity, effortless "delivering catastrophe like
   a weather report." Risk: audience waits for the rant that must never come.
2. **Jason Bateman** — the five-second "water weight" stare is his home turf.
   Risk: reads too competent; Dale must be a genuinely bad criminal.
3. **Will Forte** — can do sad-underneath better than almost any comedian; would
   need aging toward the character.

## RICKY FONTAINE (47) — coke-fueled doomed romantic

1. **Jason Mantzoukas** — announces terrible plans with TED Talk energy as a
   resting state; and the Perkins/Tommy reveal would blindside an audience that
   thinks it knows what he's for. First offer.
2. **Bobby Cannavale** — the romantic sincerity is real; "she gave me a package
   rate, because we have something" plays without a hint of sleaze. Ages perfectly.
3. **Charlie Day** — motormouth pedigree; would make Ricky more chaos-gremlin,
   less doomed romantic. Recalibrates the part younger.
4. **Danny McBride** — delusional confidence royalty; pulls the film 10% broader.
5. **Ben Foster** (dark-horse) — if we want the overdose shadow genuinely felt in
   every scene. The comedy gets scarier, the bridge scene gets unbearable.

**Chemistry rule:** Dale/Ricky pairs must read as *married*, not as a double act.
Test pairs, not singles — the audition scene is the bickering montage plus the
parking-garage silence.

## LARRY GUNDERSON (60s) — the politest crime boss in Ohio

1. **John Carroll Lynch** — Fargo DNA, enormous gentleness with a basement under
   it. "I'm not mad, I'm disappointed" would enter the culture. First offer.
2. **Tracy Letts** — the crack beat (Polish ruling #2) is his exact register:
   courtesy over a furnace.
3. **J.K. Simmons** — instantly credible menace AND pancake-dad warmth; risk is
   the audience pre-knowing he's scary (we lose the surprise of the cocoa).
4. **Richard Jenkins** — the most heartbreaking option; his Larry leads with the
   grief-frozen mama's boy and lets menace be inferred.
5. **Stephen Root** — sweater-vest native; menace via hospitality at a slightly
   higher comic pitch.

## CRYSTAL DUBOIS (38) — escort, accounting student, smartest person in the film

1. **Aubrey Plaza** — surgical honesty at conversational volume is her factory
   setting; and she can land "here we all are" as both joke and thesis. First offer.
2. **Krysten Ritter** — audit-grade stare, bone-dry; effortless "nothing barks at
   Crystal" authority.
3. **Riley Keough** — grounds the character's economy (pricing everything,
   including feelings) in something warmer and sadder.
4. **Natasha Lyonne** — maximal flavor; would want to pull the register from NY
   to Midwest, but the world-weariness is bought, not acted.
5. **DeWanda Wise** — precision plus warmth; the parking-garage monologue in her
   hands becomes a closing argument.

## DOTTIE (74) — owner of The Squeaky Wheel

1. **Margo Martindale** — loving abuse incarnate; the "she's counted every one of
   these for thirty years" hold needs zero dialogue with her. First and only
   offer if she says yes.
2. **Dale Dickey** — the most authentically Rust Belt face in American film.
3. **Jean Smart** — warmer, wittier Dottie; the bar becomes even more the heart.
4. **Ann Dowd** — moral center with dockworker arms; grief notes for free.

## THE JESSUP TWINS (DARRYL & DARRYL, 30s)

Cast as a comedy PAIR, not two singles — the double act is the character.
1. **Tim Robinson (Short Darryl) + Conner O'Malley (Tall Darryl)** — the
   incandescent "IT DOESN'T EVEN SCAN! THE METER'S ALL WRONG!" is a Robinson
   aria; O'Malley's unhinged sincerity makes the truck-cab surrender at Dale's
   knock genuinely great. Risk: scene theft — contain to their scripted beats.
2. **Beck Bennett + Kyle Mooney** — pre-built chemistry, softer menace.
3. **Two actual unknown brothers from Midwest casting calls** — cheapest and
   possibly truest; the doc talking-head in front of camo curtains needs a face
   nobody has seen before.
