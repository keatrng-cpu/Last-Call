# LAST CALL — Storyboard: The Bridge (Moral Choice Beat #3)
*EXT. MAUMEE RIVER BRIDGE - DAWN. Text boards + AI image-generation prompts.*

The scene in one sentence: two men on a steel bridge at dawn decide not to take
the money, and the film's first beautiful light watches them do it.

Grammar note: this is the convergence scene — Language A's beauty finally shooting
Language B's people sincerely. Every panel is composed prestige; nothing is a joke
frame until Panel 9.

---

## PANELS

**P1 — THE ARRIVAL (drone, high wide)**
Box truck crests the old steel truss bridge and stops at the apex. River below,
running toward everywhere else. First golden-hour frame of the film.
*Cut intent: hold 6 sec; engine ticks, no score yet.*

**P2 — THE GEOMETRY (ground wide, 24mm — THE FRAME)**
The whole choice in one composition: the two men at the rail, tiny against the
truss; the goose-lady painting propped between them. Frame LEFT (behind them):
the road back to Maumee Flats. Frame RIGHT (ahead): the interstate south.
Crystal's Camry parked at far right edge, Crystal leaning on the door, arms
folded — present, not deciding. This frame returns at P8 and P11; it is the
scene's home.

**P3 — THE PAINTING (insert, 65mm)**
The stern farm woman with the goose, propped against the rail, appraisal letter
taped to the back lifting slightly in the wind. She stares at camera. She is, in
this light, weirdly Larry-like.

**P4 — RICKY SELLS FLORIDA (single, 50mm, warm side)**
Ricky lit full golden hour — the dream side of the bridge. "We're MORONS, Dale!
We'd FIT!" Big, alive, arms going. The most flattering light Ricky gets in the
whole film, spent on his worst idea.

**P5 — "AND EMMA?" (single, 50mm, cold side)**
Dale, back to the sunrise, face half in shadow. The line lands. *Cut intent:
cut to P6 a beat EARLY — deflation, not deadpan; different timing rule.*

**P6 — RICKY DEFLATES HALF AN INCH (two-shot, 40mm, over the rail)**
Camera outside the rail looking back at both men, river far below their elbows.
"...She could visit Dominic." The joke and the surrender in the same breath.

**P7 — DALE'S MATH (slow push, 50mm → tighter)**
The fifteen-hours-sober speech. One slow push-in — the deadpan grammar spent on
sincerity for the first time. Flask never appears; his hands grip the rail
instead. *Score enters here: single sustained note, the dawn-drive cue's first
seed.*

**P8 — "I THREW THE PARTY, DALE." (return to P2 wide)**
Tommy's confession plays in the WIDE. Radical choice: the most intimate line in
the film in the most distant frame — the bridge holds them, the way the bar
holds them. Wind, river, two small men, one terrible little laugh.

**P9 — THE GOOSE LADY JUDGES (insert, matching P3)**
Same insert as P3, but now a slow 2-inch push on her disapproving face. The
film's only joke inside the scene — one panel of comic relief before the choice
lands. *Ricky exhales thirty years offscreen.*

**P10 — THE HANDOFF (two-shot, 40mm)**
Ricky hands Dale the painting. "We're criminals, Dale. We negotiate." Dale's slow
smile — the first real one of the film. Board it: the smile is the panel's
subject, not the painting.

**P11 — "OPERATION GOOSE LADY." (return to P2 wide, final)**
Ricky already walking to the truck. Dale a beat behind, painting under his arm.
The truck's nose points LEFT — back toward Maumee Flats. The geometry of P2
resolved: they chose the frame-left road. Crystal pushes off her car door.
*Cut intent: this wide dissolves into the dawn-drive needle-drop.*

**P12 — THE DRIVE (drone, following)**
Truck and Camry cross the bridge toward home, long dawn shadows, the one
needle-drop of the film swelling. The doc has been wasting drones on tarps all
movie; this is what the drone was for.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- One quiet dawn hour is the whole window; board discipline matters — P2/P8/P11
  are the same setup (shoot all three passes consecutively), P3/P9 the same
  insert. Realistic count: 7 setups in ~75 minutes with a rehearsed crew.
- Traffic control both ends; the honking-traffic beat from the earlier highway
  fork does NOT recur here — the bridge is empty, that's the point.
- Safety: nobody sits or leans OVER the rail; P6's camera position is a crane arm
  outside the rail, not an operator.
- Backup weather plan: an overcast dawn actually grades beautifully toward the
  film's palette — shoot it and warm it in the grade; do not lose the day.
