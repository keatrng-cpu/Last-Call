# LAST CALL — Shot List: Act One (+ Set-Piece Coverage Index)
*Master Director Connector v1.0. Language A = docuseries grammar; Language B = reality grammar (see VISUAL_BIBLE.md).*

Numbering: Scene.Setup. Lenses assume Super 35 spherical. All Language B handheld
unless marked sticks. Timing notes are cutting intent, not set law.

---

## SC 1 — EXT. MAUMEE FLATS - NIGHT (DOCUSERIES) — Language A

- **1.1** Drone, 24mm, slow forward glide over dead main street toward the one lit
  sign (THE SQUEAKY WHEEL). Prestige grade, synth score. 30 sec, no cuts — let the
  self-seriousness marinate.
- **1.2** Drone, push past a boarded storefront, rack to a flapping tarp. The doc
  thinks this is meaningful. It is a tarp.

## SC 2 — INT. TALKING HEAD: SHERIFF PACZKI — Language A

- **2.1** Sticks, 40mm, dead-center symmetry, flag soft in background, shallow DOF.
  ONE setup only — talking heads never get coverage; the cut does the joke.
  Hold the "Nobody wants to do the paperwork" beat two full seconds before cutting.

## SC 3 — EXT. WALMART PARKING LOT - NIGHT — Language B

*The hard cut from 2.1 to 3.1 is the film's thesis statement: prestige → drizzle.*

- **3.1** Handheld wide, 28mm, sodium vapor, drizzle in the lights. F-150 mid-lot.
  Ricky "lookout" posture in foreground, sparks under truck background. Flat,
  ugly, honest.
- **3.2** Low creeper-cam under truck, 35mm: Dale sawing, sparks. Practical work
  light only. Uncomfortable, cramped — his office.
- **3.3** MED Ricky, 35mm: the wave to the passing car, then the key bump. Do not
  cut around the bump; one shot, matter-of-fact — vice as routine (see Polish
  ruling #3).
- **3.4** Two-shot when Dale slides out with the converter held like a caught fish.
  Sticks — the film's first stable frame, granted the instant they "succeed."
- **3.5** SLOW PUSH-IN, 50mm, on Dale's dead face for "Forty bucks. Split two
  ways." Hold a beat past comfort. First deployment of the deadpan push — teach
  the audience the grammar here.

## SC 4 — TALKING HEAD: MRS. HALVERSEN — Language A

- **4.1** Sticks, 40mm, doily centered behind her. Same symmetry as 2.1 — the doc
  frames a retired teacher like a cartel witness. Hold on "A crier."

## SC 5 — INT. THE SQUEAKY WHEEL - NIGHT — Language B, AMBER

- **5.1** Steadicam entrance drift, 32mm, following the beer to Dale's stool.
  Warmest frame of the film so far. Establish geography: bar, back booth, open-mic
  corner (Kevin), back window to the dumpster.
- **5.2** MED Dottie across the bar, 40mm. Her setups are always slightly low —
  she is the authority in this room.
- **5.3** INSERT, 65mm: the flask refilling the empty shot glass. Then **5.4** MCU
  Dottie watching, one beat, saying nothing. This pair recurs all film; keep the
  framing identical each time (ritual-gag rule).
- **5.5** Kevin corner wide, 28mm, USB mic and laptop. Shoot him slightly too
  small in frame — nine followers of composition.
- **5.6** THE MR. BUSINESS FRAME: sticks, 50mm, through the back window, raccoon
  centered atop dumpster, still, judging. This exact frame, height, and lens every
  time he appears, all film. Toast coverage: wide from behind the bar, glasses up.
- **5.7** THE BECCA BEAT: move to a 50mm two-shot Dottie/Dale, and for exactly four
  lines let the bar noise fall away in the mix. No push-in here — deadpan gets the
  push, grief gets stillness. Dale's hand pausing on the glass is the cut point in.
- **5.8** Ricky leaning in to ruin it ("You're always 'you know,' Dale") — cut to a
  loose three-shot; comedy re-enters the frame with him.

## SC 6 — INT. SQUEAKY WHEEL - BACK BOOTH — Language B

- **6.1** Overhead God-shot, 28mm, of the crayon map on the table. Nobody addresses
  the crayon; neither does the camera — no insert, no emphasis. The joke is the
  refusal.
- **6.2** Booth two-shot coverage, 40mm cross-singles for the codename negotiation.
  Cut to **5.6 Mr. Business frame** for "No offense." Same frame. Always the same
  frame.

## SC 7 — EXT. STOR-MOR SELF STORAGE - NIGHT — Language B (until the doors open)

- **7.1** WIDE sticks, 24mm: rows of identical orange doors, buzzing fluorescents.
  Two idiots creeping in cartoon-burglar posture, tiny in frame. Hold the wide for
  the whole creep — sneaking is funnier at a distance.
- **7.2** Singles for the 141/114 map debate, 40mm. Flat shot/reverse — a
  procedural argument shot like a procedural.
- **7.3** INSERT crayon map, Dale's thumb over the numbers. The only map insert in
  the film; it earns it by being wrong.
- **7.4** Bolt cutters SNAP + door RATTLE: cut to WIDE 7.1 framing for the
  headlight-deer freeze. Three full seconds. Nothing happens. Comedy of duration.

## SC 8 — INT. STORAGE UNIT 141 — transition to Language A

*The unit interior is where the doc's grammar invades reality: this is the one
place the prestige style is telling the truth.*

- **8.1** Flashlight-motivated dolly, 32mm, sweeping shrink-wrap and banker's
  boxes. Score creeps in — first non-doc score cue in reality.
- **8.2** 65mm INSERT: the urn, alone on the folding table, lit like a saint
  (single hard source, gentle top-light halo — one 300W fixture through a snoot).
- **8.3** Duffel open: MCU Dale, flashlight shake. **8.4** INSERT banded cash.
- **8.5** QUICK-CUT series (chaos rule): hands stuffing cash / Ricky lifting the
  urn / and END on **8.6**, locked-off corner-mounted security-cam angle, red
  blink, our two heroes small and oblivious. The security cam is Language A's
  eyeball inside the scene — foreshadow by format (degrade to CCTV texture).

## SC 9 — TALKING HEAD: GARY — Language A

- **9.1** Sticks, 40mm — but compose the Dairy Queen with the car-shaped hole
  visible through the window behind him, in focus. The background does the
  cross-examination.

## SC 10 — INT. SQUEAKY WHEEL BACK ROOM - NIGHT (THE COUNT + MORAL CHOICE #1)

- **10.1** Single bulb practical. WIDE, 28mm: cash on card table, mop buckets, Bud
  Light mirror. Squalor as counting house.
- **10.2** Cross-singles 50mm for the "how much did you spend" interrogation.
  Ricky's single slightly tighter than Dale's — he's the one sweating.
- **10.3** Dale sits on the mop bucket: WIDE. Do not go close. The saddest chair in
  cinema plays at a distance, held.
- **10.4** MORAL CHOICE #1: symmetrical two-shot, 35mm, money and urn between
  them, the swinging bulb alternating light across their faces (grip swings it on
  a fishpole, 4-second period). Hold the frame for the whole options speech — the
  wide holds the decision (moral-beat rule). Cut in only on "An un-robbery."
- **10.5** The handshake + codename: 65mm insert of the shake, played dead
  sincere. Score allowed one noble French horn, immediately cut off by the smash.

## SC 11 — EXT./INT. STOR-MOR - NIGHT (THE UN-ROBBERY) — full Language A parody

- **11.1** Gliding Steadicam down the unit row, 32mm, tense score — Ocean's
  grammar over men returning money.
- **11.2** Split diopter: padlock foreground / Ricky's ski mask background.
- **11.3** THE URN DROP: 120fps slow-mo, 85mm, score swelling operatically as the
  urn tumbles — Dale's dive, fingertips — impact. **SMASH to 24fps, score dead,
  room tone only.** Ash cloud drifts. Hold the wide.
- **11.4** Two singles staring down at Evelyn: flattest 50mm frames in the film
  for "...Ope." / "That's Larry's mom."
- **11.5** DUKE SEQUENCE (chaos quick-cuts): truck peeling out / mantel + Labrador
  photo / the kiss, "forgive me" / peeling back / **11.6** WIDE sticks, 24mm: two
  grown men on their knees at 3 A.M. funneling a Labrador into a crime boss's
  mother. Longest wide of the sequence. Do not cut in. This is the poster of Act
  One.
- **11.7** LARRY'S ARRIVAL: on "Fellas," FREEZE the men in frame; SLOW PAN (not a
  cut — a pan, the room turning its head) to Larry, sweater vest, thermos, two
  spare cups, flanked by Flapjack polos. **11.8** MCU the thermos unscrewing —
  mixed like a gunshot. Nothing in the film so far as frightening as the cocoa.

## SC 12 — INT. FLAPJACK CABIN - CORNER BOOTH - NIGHT — THE LARRY VISE

- **12.1** ONE motorized-slider push-in, 35mm→ effectively tighter, sub-perceptual
  speed, whole scene, on the booth three-shot. This is the master and the scene
  lives in it; cut to coverage sparingly and always return tighter than you left.
- **12.2** Coverage: Larry single 50mm (he gets the cleanest, kindest light in the
  film — that's the horror); guys' dirty two-shot 40mm, cramped against the window.
- **12.3** INSERT: Ricky under stress quietly coloring the kids' placemat. One
  shot, no comment.
- **12.4** THE CRACK (Polish ruling #2): on "My mother came back... heavier," the
  slider STOPS. Diner ambience drops out of the mix. Larry's hand flattens on the
  table. Two seconds of the wrong face. Ambience fades back; slider resumes;
  boysenberry. No added dialogue — sound and stillness only.
- **12.5** "Water weight." — cut to Dale's five-second dead turn to Ricky, 65mm,
  the longest deadpan hold in the movie. THE TRAILER SHOT. Protect it from the
  editor's mercy; it needs all five seconds.
- **12.6** Enforcer closing his eyes in prayer: 50mm single, two frames longer
  than reasonable.
- **12.7** Larry's exit line + Brenda signal: end the scene at the push-in's
  tightest point — the booth now a cell.

## SC 13 — TALKING HEAD: BRENDA — Language A

- **13.1** Standard doc frame; she crosses herself with the coffee pot. One take
  choice: keep the pot IN frame throughout so the gesture isn't a surprise cut.

## SET-PIECE COVERAGE INDEX (Acts Two & Three — full lists on request)

- **Bickering montage (truck):** hood-mount 2-shot master + door-mount singles;
  every cut enters mid-argument; the 10-second cornfield silence is one static
  hood-mount hold.
- **Sir Cheese-A-Lot / casino intercut:** cut on motion-matches (Emma bounces /
  dice bounce; candles / chips; Dale kneels / Ricky bows). Dale's flask beat at
  the ball pit: 50mm, foley-forward (cap squeak), longest hold in the film.
  His exit: widest frame of Act 2, animatronic mouse singing him out.
- **Parking garage (Crystal's scene — protected):** sodium top-light, the empty
  parking space framed like a grave in the master; Crystal delivers the thesis in
  a single slow 40mm push that stops the moment she says "here we all are."
- **Jessup compound heist:** crane reveal; split-screen triptych (Ricky's Say
  Anything laptop / Dale's polite knock / Crystal at the fuse box); Tall Darryl's
  POV of sober Dale through cab glass shot as one beat of horror film; drone
  finally earns its keep on the ATV sinking.
- **The bridge (Moral Choice #3):** ONE WIDE holds the whole choice — road home
  behind, interstate ahead, Crystal present but not deciding. No coverage until
  "And Emma?"
- **Finale one-take (Squeaky Wheel, three months later):** Steadicam, 32mm,
  ~2.5 minutes, choreographed like a stunt: Emma's converter tour → Becca laughing
  → Crystal's register + diploma → Kevin's tribute → Larry's booth → Mr. Business
  vest toast → land on the bar for the flask/chip reveal and the last deadpan.
  Rehearse on a marked floorplan a full day before shooting.
