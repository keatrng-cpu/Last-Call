# LAST CALL

**Written by FableForge Cinematic Architect**
*Based on a premise by the User*

---

## LOGLINE

Two washed-up best friends — a deadpan alcoholic "mastermind" and a coke-fueled romantic who loves escorts and bad decisions — accidentally rob the politest crime boss in Ohio and must pull off the only heist they're qualified for: breaking in to give everything back.

---

## SYNOPSIS

In the dying Rust Belt town of Maumee Flats, Ohio, Dale Kowalski and Ricky Fontaine have spent thirty years perfecting the art of the pathetic crime — copper wire, catalytic converters, a vending machine they've robbed so many times the company just leaves it unlocked now. When they boost the wrong storage unit, they end up with $88,000 belonging to Larry Gunderson, a crime boss who runs the tri-county underworld from a family pancake restaurant and has never once raised his voice. Worse: Ricky's already blown $11,000 of it, and Dale's dog urn got mixed up with the ashes of Larry's sainted mother. Forced to work off the debt with one simple truck delivery, the guys instead detour through a casino bender, a granddaughter's birthday party, a diss track that goes locally viral, and a rival crew of meth-adjacent morons — until the only way out is the one thing neither man has ever tried: showing up sober, honest, and on time. A comedy about petty crime, weaponized Midwestern politeness, and what you owe the person who watched you ruin yourself and stayed anyway.

## CHARACTER BIBLE

**DALE KOWALSKI** — 54. Ex-machinist. Face like a foreclosed house: still standing, but you wouldn't get a loan on it. Flannel in all weather. Carries his late father's flask like other men carry a wedding ring.
- *Personality:* Deadpan, joyless-sounding, secretly sentimental. Believes he is a criminal genius. Is not.
- *Humor style:* Bone-dry understatement. Delivers catastrophic news like a weather report.
- *Arc:* From drinking to forget his family → getting sober long enough to be terrifying → choosing his daughter over the score.
- *Moral flaw/strength:* Flaw — the bottle is his co-pilot and his alibi. Strength — he has never once, in thirty years, left Ricky behind. Not even when he should have.
- *Key relationships:* Ricky (brother in everything but paperwork), Becca (estranged daughter, Toledo), Dottie (bar owner, surrogate disappointed mom).

**RICKY FONTAINE** — 47. "In sales." Gold chain, pit-stained Tommy Bahama shirt, sunglasses indoors, cocaine confidence with a decaf bank account. Falls in love approximately hourly.
- *Personality:* Doomed optimist. Treats every escort like a soulmate and every bump like a business meeting.
- *Humor style:* Motormouth delusion. Announces terrible plans with TED Talk energy.
- *Arc:* From playing chicken with the same overdose that took his little brother → admitting the coke and the girls were just noise to drown out one specific silence → choosing a real person (Crystal) and a real life (co-owning a bar he will absolutely still steal from).
- *Moral flaw/strength:* Flaw — he'd sell your kidney to buy you a birthday present. Strength — he genuinely believes in people, especially the ones who've stopped believing in themselves.
- *Key relationships:* Dale (the only constant), Crystal (escort he's paid to *talk* to for a year), the ghost of his brother Tommy.

**LARRY GUNDERSON** — 60s. Regional crime boss. Owns fourteen Flapjack Cabin franchises. Sweater vests. Never swears, never yells, tips 25%.
- *Personality:* Aggressively polite. His "I'm not mad, I'm disappointed" has ended careers, marriages, and at least one man's ability to enjoy pancakes.
- *Humor style:* Menace via hospitality. Offers you a booster seat before ruining your life.
- *Arc:* Grief-frozen mama's boy → a man who lets one debt go, because his mother would have liked these two idiots.
- *Moral flaw/strength:* Flaw — controls everything because he couldn't control losing her. Strength — a genuinely fair ledger. Larry never takes more than he's owed.

**CRYSTAL DUBOIS** — 38. Escort, night-school accounting student, smartest person in the film by a margin that should embarrass everyone.
- *Personality:* Unsentimental, hilarious, done with everyone's nonsense — except, inconveniently, Ricky's.
- *Humor style:* Surgical honesty. Says the quiet part at conversational volume.
- *Arc:* From billing Ricky hourly → realizing she's been on the world's longest, weirdest first date → choosing him at his most broke and most honest.
- *Moral flaw/strength:* Flaw — protects herself by pricing everything, including feelings. Strength — sees exactly who people are and stays anyway.

**DOTTIE** — 74. Owner of The Squeaky Wheel, the last real bar in Maumee Flats. Beehive hairdo, arms like a dockworker.
- *Personality:* Runs a tab for men she's known since they were altar boys. Threatens their lives daily. Would burn the town down for them.
- *Humor style:* Loving abuse.
- *Arc:* Static and perfect — the moral center disguised as a bartender.

**THE JESSUP TWINS (DARRYL & DARRYL)** — 30s. Rival petty criminals. Yes, both named Darryl; their mother "liked the name and didn't see the problem." Meth-adjacent, camo everything, furious that Dale and Ricky keep getting credit for crimes the Jessups actually committed.
- *Humor style:* Violent stupidity. The threat is real; the execution never is.

**MR. BUSINESS** — Age unknown. A raccoon who lives in the dumpster behind The Squeaky Wheel. Sacred. Do not touch Mr. Business. Regulars believe he predicts economic downturns.

## THEME & TONE BIBLE

- **Core question:** *What do you owe the person who watched you ruin yourself — and stayed?*
- **Tone:** *It's Always Sunny* escalation grounded in *Fargo* politeness and Coen-brothers heart. Vice played for laughs, but grief played straight — every joke about the drinking and the coke is a floorboard over something real, and Act 3 pulls the boards up.
- **The Docuseries Frame:** The film is intercut with a cheesy true-crime streaming doc, *RUST BELT OUTLAWS: The Maumee Flats Crime Wave* — dramatic drone shots, ominous synth, townsfolk talking heads who describe two idiots stealing lawn ornaments as though narrating the fall of Rome. The doc is our Greek chorus and our laugh engine: it either wildly overrates the guys ("criminal masterminds") or brutally underrates them ("I taught both of them. D-minus students. Both of them."). It also delivers exposition so the screenplay never has to.
- **Rules of comedy:** Consequences are always shown, never explained. Midwestern politeness is a weapon. Nobody in this town reacts appropriately to anything.

**KEVIN**

Mr. Business.

**DOTTIE**
(to Dale, low; the real scene begins)

Stor-Mor Self Storage. Route 9. Unit 114. Guy at the scrapyard says the renter died, nobody's claimed it, and the padlock's older than Dottie.

**EXT. STOR-MOR SELF STORAGE - NIGHT**

*[Wide shot: rows of identical orange doors under buzzing fluorescents. Two idiots creep along the row with the sneaking posture of cartoon burglars, which is somehow louder than walking.]*

They stop at a unit. Dale checks his crayon map. Ricky checks nothing.

**DALE**
One-fourteen.

**RICKY**
(pointing at the door)
This says one-forty-one.

**DALE**
(squinting at the map)
The map says one-fourteen.

**RICKY**

**DALE**

**RICKY**

**DALE**
The numbers probably got transposed. One-fourteen, one-forty-one. Same numbers. Legally identical.

**RICKY**
(nodding, fully convinced)
That's just math.

**INT. STORAGE UNIT 141 - CONTINUOUS**

*[Flashlight beams sweep: not dusty estate junk. Clean. Organized. Shrink-wrapped. A wall of banker's boxes. A duffel bag. And on a small folding table, all alone, lit like a saint — a brass URN.]*

**RICKY**

More than forty dollars.

**GARY**

CUT TO:

**INT. THE SQUEAKY WHEEL - BACK ROOM - NIGHT**

*[Cash stacked on a card table under a single bulb. Dottie's back room: mop buckets, a Bud Light mirror, and now eighty-eight thousand dollars.]*

**DALE**
(counting, quietly losing his mind)

**RICKY**

**DALE**

**RICKY**

**INT. DOCUSERIES TALKING HEAD - SHERIFF PACZKI - DAY**

**SHERIFF PACZKI**

**SHERIFF PACZKI (CONT'D)**

CUT TO:

**INT. THE SQUEAKY WHEEL - BACK ROOM - CONTINUOUS**

**DALE**
Okay. Okay. Nobody knows it was us. We put it back. Every dollar. Tonight.

*[Silence. A very specific silence. Dale turns slowly. Ricky is doing the face of a man about to describe a car accident he caused.]*

**DALE (CONT'D)**
Ricky. Why is the bag lighter than eighty-eight.

**RICKY**
Okay. So. Funny story—

**DALE**
No.

**RICKY**

**DALE**
How much.

**RICKY**
(small)
Eleven.

**DALE**
Eleven HUNDRED?

**RICKY**
(smaller)
...Units of thousand.

**DALE**

**RICKY**
(counting on fingers, defensive)
Okay — I paid off my tab here, which, thank you, that's basically an investment in a local business. I bought Kevin a real microphone so the diss tracks about us would at least SOUND professional, because I have pride, Dale. And I booked Crystal.

**DALE**
For eleven thousand dollars?!

**RICKY**
Through October!

*[Beat.]*

**RICKY (CONT'D)**
(with dignity)
She gave me a package rate. Because we have something.

**DALE**
She has a BILLING structure, Ricky. What you have is a subscription.

**RICKY**
(pointing, genuinely hurt)
Hey. Ope — no. You don't get to talk about Crystal. You're fine — you're FINE — you son of a bitch.

*[MORAL CHOICE BEAT #1 — frame it like one: the money on the table, the urn between them, the two men on opposite sides. The bulb overhead swings slightly, alternating light between their faces.]*

**DALE**
Option one: we run. Seventy-seven grand, Florida, new names.

**RICKY**
I've always felt like a "Dominic."

**DALE**
Option two: we put back what's left, and we spend the rest of our lives hoping Larry Gunderson never learns math.

**RICKY**
He owns fourteen restaurants, Dale. The man can count.

**DALE**
(standing; the plan-face)
Then there's option three.

**RICKY**
I'm listening.

**RICKY**

An un-robbery.

**DALE**
An un-robbery.

**RICKY**

"Operation Take-Backsies."

**DALE**

NO.

**LARRY (O.S.)**

**LARRY (CONT'D)**

**INT. FLAPJACK CABIN - CORNER BOOTH - NIGHT**

*[Fluorescent breakfast-restaurant cheer at 4 A.M. Laminated menus. A kids' placemat in front of Ricky that he is, under stress, quietly coloring. The two polo-shirt enforcers stand by the door with the serenity of men who have buried things in cornfields.]*

**LARRY**

**DALE**
Mr. Gunderson—

**LARRY**

**RICKY**
(swallowing)

**LARRY**

**LARRY (CONT'D)**

**DALE**

**LARRY (CONT'D)**

**RICKY**
(instantly)
Water weight.

**LARRY**
(setting down his coffee cup with terrible gentleness)
I'm not mad, fellas.

**DALE**
(under his breath)
Oh no.

**LARRY**
I'm disappointed.

**RICKY**
(genuine panic)
Larry, no—

**LARRY**
Because you know what you did when you brought that money back? You showed me character. Confused, D-minus character — but character. And that presents me with an opportunity, because it happens I need two men with character and no visible connection to me to drive a truck to Toledo on Saturday.

**DALE**
What's in the truck?

**LARRY**
My mother's estate. Furniture. Antiques. Her whole house, packed with love, going to my sister. She never threw a thing away — the estate fellow tagged the lot, said one or two pieces might be worth real money.

*[He straightens a napkin that was already straight.]*

**LARRY (CONT'D)**
I couldn't bring myself to read his little stickers.

(brightening; back to business)

**LARRY (CONT'D)**
And you two are going to drive it there, unload it, and come straight back — no stops, no detours, no creative interpretations — and when you do, the eleven thousand is forgiven, and the matter of the urn...

*[He looks out the window. A muscle in his cheek.]*

**LARRY (CONT'D)**
...becomes something we never speak of again.

**DALE**
And if the truck doesn't make it to Toledo?

*[Larry smiles. He signals the waitress.]*

**LARRY**
Brenda? Boysenberry for my friends.

(back to Dale, still smiling)

**LARRY (CONT'D)**
Then I'll be more than disappointed, Dale.

**BRENDA**
I've worked that corner booth nine years. When Larry says "more than disappointed"...

(she crosses herself with a coffee pot)

**BRENDA (CONT'D)**
...you comp the table and you go home to your family.

**ACT TWO**

**EXT. THE SQUEAKY WHEEL - SATURDAY MORNING**

*[A 26-foot box truck idles out front, "GUNDERSON MOVING & PANCAKES" stenciled on the side. Dale loads a cooler. Dottie stands on the stoop with her arms crossed like a general seeing off two privates she does not believe in.]*

**DOTTIE**
Toledo's ninety minutes. You've got a full tank and God's daylight. Explain to me how you two screw this up.

**DALE**
We won't.

**DOTTIE**
Dale.

## DIRECTOR'S VISION NOTES

- **Visual grammar:** Two competing film languages at war — the *docuseries* (drones, ominous synth, prestige color grade) versus *reality* (flat, handheld, sodium-lit, unglamorous). The joke of the whole movie lives in that gap: the legend versus two guys in a Walmart parking lot. As the guys become genuinely braver in Act 3, the two styles converge — the heist at the Jessup compound is the first time reality gets to look as cool as the documentary claims it is.
- **Comedic timing rules:** Deadpan gets the slow push-in and the extra beat (Dale's five-second stare after "water weight" is the trailer shot). Chaos gets quick cuts. Larry's scenes get one continuous, imperceptible push-in per scene — politeness as a tightening frame.
- **Color:** The town is desaturated grays and browns; The Squeaky Wheel is the only warm amber in the film until the Act 3 dawn drive — the first golden hour, earned, not given.
- **Sound design:** Larry is always scored with gentle diner ambience — clinking cutlery, soft-rock radio — even when he's threatening lives. The scariest sounds in the film are a thermos being unscrewed and syrup being poured.
- **Music:** Kevin's diss tracks function as a Greek chorus and improve in production quality as the plot escalates (the $11,000 microphone is a plot-load-bearing joke). Needle-drop budget goes to one song only: the Act 3 dawn drive.
- **Set pieces:** (1) The Un-Robbery, shot as Ocean's Eleven. (2) Sir Cheese-A-Lot / casino intercut — joy and rot in parallel. (3) The Jessup compound heist — Say Anything laptop-boombox as the diversion. (4) The bridge — one wide frame holding the whole moral choice.
